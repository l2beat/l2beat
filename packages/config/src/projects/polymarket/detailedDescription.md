Polymarket is a prediction market: users trade shares in the outcomes of real-world events, and once an event resolves each share redeems for the payout reported for its outcome. The share ledger and the order-book exchanges are immutable. The collateral token, the wallets, the combination markets and the resolution path are operated or upgradeable by the protocol.

### Outcome shares

Outcome shares are ERC-1155 tokens on a conditional-token ledger that is immutable and has no admin. Anyone can lock collateral to mint a complete set of a market's outcome shares, and anyone holding a complete set can burn it back into collateral. Each market names a single resolver address at creation, and only that address can report its payouts. The report is write-once. Redemption after resolution is permissionless. Multi-outcome and combination markets add their own ledgers and adapters, which hold most of the protocol's upgradeable code.

### Collateral

Trading collateral is {{collateralSymbol}}, an upgradeable token that wraps a stablecoin one-for-one. Wrapping deposits the underlying into a vault and mints {{collateralSymbol}}; unwrapping burns it and pulls the underlying back out. Both directions are permissionless through the ramp contracts, so redemption does not depend on the operator. Admins can pause either ramp with immediate effect, and the token can be upgraded. The outcome modules of the combination-market system hold a minter role that creates {{collateralSymbol}} without depositing any underlying.

Retail deposits arrive through a hosted bridge and are credited to per-user smart wallets. The wallets execute only batches signed by their owner or an owner-authorised session signer, and direct withdrawals are restricted to the owner, so the relayer that submits transactions cannot spend user balances. All wallets share one implementation through a beacon, so a single upgrade replaces every wallet's code. A user can pause their wallet and opt out of the shared implementation. Pausing does not stop signed batches; it unlocks direct owner withdrawals after {{walletPauseDelay}}, a delay admins can raise without notice.

### Trading

The order book runs off-chain. Users sign orders stating a limit price, a permissioned operator matches them and submits the fills, and the exchange contracts verify each signature and price bound on-chain. Settlement mints a complete set, burns one, or transfers shares between the two sides. The operator controls inclusion and ordering, and can delay, reorder or refuse to match any order. It cannot invent a trade or cross an order at a worse share price than the one signed. The fee is the exception: it is supplied at settlement rather than signed by the user, and is bounded only by a maximum rate the admins configure, {{feeCeilingClause}}. Fees go to a fee-receiver multisig. Cancellation is an off-chain request, so the on-chain remedy is to pause one's own orders, effective after {{userPauseBlocks}} blocks. Combination markets are quoted through a request-for-quote flow, with the same signature-checked settlement.

### Resolution

Markets are answered by a permissioned deployment of the UMA optimistic oracle: only whitelisted addresses may request a price or propose an answer. A proposal stands unless disputed within {{oracleLiveness}}, subject to a minimum dispute window of {{oracleDisputeWindow}}. A first dispute restarts the question; a second escalates to the oracle's tokenholder vote. Settlement requires a resolver role, so a proposal never becomes final on its own. The protocol's multisig controls the pointer to the proposer whitelist, while the whitelist contract in use and the resolver role sit with a second multisig shared with the oracle's operators.

The resolution adapters give their admins a direct override. An admin can flag a market and impose an outcome — either side, or an even split — after {{adapterSafetyPeriod}} on binary markets and {{legacyAdapterSafetyPeriod}} on the older adapter still holding legacy markets, where the payout is unconstrained. For multi-outcome markets the operator contract exposes the same power with {{negRiskOperatorDelay}}, so flagging and forcing an outcome can happen in one transaction. Flagging also blocks the permissionless resolution path. Admins can pause or reset any market's resolution indefinitely.

### Governance and upgrades

A {{adminSafe}} multisig holds most admin roles. The zero-delay override on multi-outcome markets is also held by an externally owned account and a separate contract, both outside the multisig and the timelock. Through the timelock the multisig can, after {{timelockDelay}}, upgrade the collateral token, the collateral vault, the wallet factory and beacon, and every contract of the combination-market system. Its other powers carry no delay: granting and revoking roles on the timelock, pausing the ramps and the exchanges, sweeping accrued fees, appointing resolution admins, and granting the module roles that mint collateral without backing or report outcomes directly. The timelock bounds how quickly the code can change, not how quickly the multisig can use the powers the code already grants it.
