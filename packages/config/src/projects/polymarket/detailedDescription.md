Polymarket is a prediction market: users buy and sell shares in the outcomes of real-world events, and once an event resolves each share redeems for the payout reported for its outcome — for a decisive result, one unit of collateral for the winning outcome and nothing for the others. The core share ledger and the order-book exchanges are immutable; the collateral token, the wallets, the combination markets and the resolution path are operated or upgradeable by the protocol.

### Outcome shares

Outcome shares are ERC-1155 tokens on a conditional-token ledger that is immutable and has no admin. Anyone can lock collateral to mint a complete set of a market's outcome shares, and anyone holding a complete set can burn it back into collateral. Each market names a single resolver address at creation, and only that address can report the market's payouts; the report is write-once, so an outcome cannot be rewritten. Redemption after resolution is likewise permissionless. Multi-outcome and combination markets add their own ledgers and adapters on top, which is where most of the protocol's upgradeable code sits.

### Collateral

Trading collateral is {{collateralSymbol}}, an upgradeable token that wraps a stablecoin one-for-one. Wrapping deposits the underlying into a vault contract and mints {{collateralSymbol}}; unwrapping burns it and pulls the underlying back out. Both directions are open to anyone through the ramp contracts, so redemption does not depend on the operator, but admins can pause either ramp with immediate effect, and the token itself can be upgraded. The outcome modules of the combination-market system hold a minter role that can create {{collateralSymbol}} without depositing any underlying.

Retail deposits arrive through a hosted bridge and are credited to per-user smart wallets. The wallets execute only batches signed by their owner or by an owner-authorised session signer, and direct withdrawals are restricted to the owner, so the relayer that submits transactions cannot spend user balances. All wallets share one implementation through a beacon, so a single upgrade replaces every wallet's code at once. A user can pause their wallet and opt out of the shared implementation; pausing does not stop signed batches, it unlocks direct owner withdrawals after {{walletPauseDelay}}, a delay admins can raise without notice.

### Trading

The order book runs off-chain. Users sign orders that state a limit price, and a permissioned operator matches them and submits the fills; the exchange contracts verify each signature and each price bound on-chain, and settlement either mints a complete set, burns one, or transfers shares between the two sides. The operator controls inclusion and ordering — it can delay, reorder or refuse to match any order — but it cannot invent a trade or cross an order at a worse share price than the one signed. The fee is the exception: it is supplied at settlement rather than signed by the user and is bounded only by a maximum rate the admins configure, {{feeCeilingClause}}. Fees go to a fee-receiver multisig, not to the operator directly. Order cancellation is an off-chain request, so the on-chain remedy is to pause one's own orders, effective after {{userPauseBlocks}} blocks. Combination markets are quoted through a request-for-quote flow instead of a book, with the same signature-checked settlement.

### Resolution

Markets are answered by a permissioned deployment of the UMA optimistic oracle: only whitelisted addresses may request a price or propose an answer. A proposal stands unless disputed within a liveness window of {{oracleLiveness}}, subject to a minimum dispute window of {{oracleDisputeWindow}}; a first dispute restarts the question and a second escalates to the oracle's tokenholder vote. Settlement also requires a resolver role, so a proposal never becomes final on its own. The pointer to the proposer whitelist is controlled by the protocol's multisig, while the whitelist contract in use and the resolver role sit with a second multisig shared with the oracle's operators.

The resolution adapters also give their admins a direct override: an admin can flag a market and impose an outcome of their choosing — either side, or an even split — after {{adapterSafetyPeriod}} on binary markets and after {{legacyAdapterSafetyPeriod}} on the older adapter still holding legacy markets, where the payout is unconstrained. For multi-outcome markets the operator contract exposes the same power with {{negRiskOperatorDelay}}, so flagging a market and forcing its outcome can happen in a single transaction; flagging also blocks the permissionless resolution path in the meantime. Admins can additionally pause or reset any market's resolution indefinitely.

### Governance and upgrades

A {{adminSafe}} multisig is the root of most control, though not all of it: the zero-delay override on multi-outcome markets is also held by an externally owned account and a separate contract outside the multisig and the timelock. Acting through the timelock the multisig can, after {{timelockDelay}}, upgrade the collateral token, the collateral vault, the wallet factory and beacon, and every contract of the combination-market system. Many of its other powers carry no delay: granting and revoking roles on the timelock itself, pausing the ramps and the exchanges, sweeping accrued fees, appointing resolution admins, and granting the module roles that can mint collateral without backing or push outcomes directly. The timelock therefore bounds how quickly the code can change, not how quickly the multisig can act on the powers the code already grants it.
