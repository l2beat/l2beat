Frankencoin is an oracle-free, collateralized stablecoin protocol issuing ZCHF, a token tracking the Swiss franc. All of its contracts are immutable and there are no admin keys, multisigs, or upgradeable proxies: every privileged action in the system is either permissionless or gated by a hardcoded quorum check against the holders of its pool share token (FPS). Instead of a price oracle, the protocol relies on economic games — anyone can challenge a position's claimed collateral price in an onchain auction — and on veto-based governance, where changes take effect only if no qualified shareholder objects during a waiting period.

### Minting ZCHF

The ZCHF token itself is a minimal ERC20 with a plug-in architecture: it keeps a registry of *minter* modules that may mint and burn ZCHF at will and book profits and losses against the shared reserve. Anyone can suggest a new minter by paying a non-refundable application fee of at least {{minFee}} ZCHF; the suggestion becomes active after an application period of at least {{minApplicationPeriod}} unless a qualified FPS holder (2% of votes) vetoes it. This veto is the system's main line of defense — once a minter's application period has passed, it can never be removed, and a malicious minter could mint unlimited ZCHF.

The main minter is the MintingHub (V2), through which anyone can open a collateralized minting position with an arbitrary ERC20 collateral (worth at least 5,000 ZCHF) against a {{openingFee}} ZCHF opening fee, or clone an existing position. New positions and clones can themselves be vetoed by qualified FPS holders during their initialization period. Once live, a position's owner can mint ZCHF against the collateral at a self-declared liquidation price, paying interest equal to the system leadrate (currently {{leadrate}}%) plus the position's fixed risk premium, and contributing a share of each mint to the minter reserve. Solvency is enforced without oracles: anyone who believes a position's liquidation price is set too high can start a challenge by posting collateral, which triggers a descending-price auction. If the auction clears below the claimed price, the position is liquidated and the challenger earns a {{challengerReward}}% reward; if it clears above, the challenge is averted and the challenger's collateral is simply sold at the claimed price. A PositionRoller helper (itself a registered minter) lets owners roll debt and collateral from an expiring position into a new one in one transaction.

Further minters are the stablecoin bridges, which mint ZCHF 1:1 against other Swiss franc stablecoins held in escrow — currently VNX Franc (VCHF, up to 2M ZCHF) and AllUnity CHF (CHFAU, up to 10M ZCHF); the original bootstrap bridge for CryptoFranc (XCHF) has expired. Each bridge has a hard cap and a one-year minting horizon after which it must be re-approved through the minter application process. A depeg or freeze of a bridged stablecoin would directly impair ZCHF backing up to the respective bridge limit.

### Governance: Equity and FPS

The Equity contract holds the system's entire ZCHF reserve — both the minter reserve contributed by position owners and the equity buffer that absorbs losses and accumulates fees. It is capitalized permissionlessly: anyone can buy FPS pool shares at a price of {{valuationFactor}}x the underlying equity per share, and redeem them after a minimum holding period of {{minHoldingDuration}}. Voting power is not just proportional to shares but to shares multiplied by holding duration, so long-term holders dominate governance. Any holder — alone or with delegated support — controlling 2% of all votes is *qualified* and can: veto minter applications, deny new minting positions, propose interest rate changes, govern the cross-chain token pool configuration, and (if equity ever falls below 1,000 ZCHF after covering losses) restructure the cap table by wiping out passive shareholders. A wrapper token (WFPS) exists for FPS; qualified holders can halve the wrapper's accumulated holding duration, which delays WFPS holders' redemption rights.

### Savings and the Savings Vault

Frankencoin has two live savings modules that pay interest on deposited ZCHF, funded by minting against the equity reserve (savings interest is booked as a loss to FPS holders, while position interest is booked as their profit). The original module (SavingsV2) doubles as the system's *leadrate* source — its rate (currently {{leadrate}}%) is what MintingHubV2 positions pay on top of their risk premium. The current Savings module pays a separately governed rate (currently {{savingsRate}}%) and supports optional referral fees of up to 25% of collected interest for frontends. In both modules, rate changes are proposed by qualified FPS holders and can be applied by anyone after a 7-day delay (a counter-proposal cancels them), and fresh deposits are subject to a 3-day interest delay.

The SavingsVault (svZCHF) is an ERC4626 tokenized wrapper around the current Savings module: deposited ZCHF is forwarded to the module and interest auto-compounds into the share price. The vault's ownership has been renounced, so its only privileged function — activating a referral-fee skim on the vault's interest — can never be called, making it fully immutable. svZCHF holders bear the same risks as direct savers: interest stops if governance sets the rate to zero, and deposits are ZCHF claims on a system whose ultimate backstop is the equity reserve.

### Multichain expansion

ZCHF expands to other chains via Chainlink CCIP: a BurnMintTokenPool (a registered minter) burns ZCHF on Ethereum and mints it on supported chains and vice versa, and helper contracts push the savings rate and FPS voting-power snapshots to the bridged deployments, whose profits and losses are settled back to mainnet through a BridgeAccounting minter. The pool is administered by the CCIPAdmin contract, where qualified FPS holders propose configuration changes (7-day timelock) or an admin handover (21-day timelock), can veto pending proposals, and can set per-chain rate limits immediately. This makes Chainlink CCIP an external dependency: a compromise of the CCIP offramps could mint ZCHF on mainnet through the token pool, bounded by the configured rate limits and, ultimately, the minter-level veto governance.
